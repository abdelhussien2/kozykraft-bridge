01. Why starters die (it's almost always one of three things).

A cold kitchen: wild yeast slows way down under 68°F, so a starter on a drafty counter can look dead for days.

Quitting on day 2 or 3: the first days often show nothing, and that's exactly when people throw it out.

Tap water: chlorine slows or kills the culture; filtered or bottled water fixes it.

02. What normal looks like, day by day.

Day 1, nothing. Day 2, probably still nothing. Day 3, the first bubbles and a sour smell. Day 4, a bigger feed. Day 5 to 7, it doubles after a feeding. Some days it rests. That's not death; that's a starter.

03. Why a dehydrated culture is different.

You're not building a culture from the wild yeast that happens to be in your flour. You're waking up an established one that's been tested for viability before it was dried.
