01. It starts with USDA organic flour — not conventionally sprayed wheat

On many conventional wheat farms, glyphosate is sprayed shortly before harvest to dry the crop. Certified organic flour isn't grown that way. KozyKraft starter is cultured from USDA organic flour and filtered water — nothing else, the way bread has been made for thousands of years.

02. No "dough conditioners" or industrial additives

Read the label on a typical grocery loaf and you'll find ingredients most people can't pronounce — azodicarbonamide (not permitted in the EU), DATEM, mono- and diglycerides, calcium propionate. They're there to give factory bread a long shelf life and a pillowy texture. A loaf from a KozyKraft starter has exactly four ingredients: flour, water, salt, and time.

03. Real, slow fermentation — the way bread used to be made

Grocery bread goes from mixer to oven in about 90 minutes using commercial yeast. Real sourdough rises slowly over many hours, giving wild yeast and lactobacillus time to work through the flour. That's where the deep, tangy flavor and chewy, open crumb come from — and it's why so many people say they can taste the difference from the very first slice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the starter arrive dead?

How long until I can bake my first loaf?

You'll see first bubbles by day 3. Keep up the daily feedings and it's bake-ready in 5–7 days. Starting a starter from scratch usually takes two to three weeks.

Any tips for getting it going?

Warmth is the big one. Starters are happiest around 75–80°F, so keep yours somewhere warm and out of drafts. Feed it on schedule every day during the first week — skipping a feeding early on is the most common reason a starter stalls.

I've never baked sourdough. Is this beginner-friendly?

Yes. The pouch comes with step-by-step instructions written for first-timers, and many of our customers bake their very first loaf with it. If you get stuck, you can reach us any time.

How do I store the pouch before I start?

It's shelf-stable. Keep the sealed pouch in a cool, dry pantry until you're ready to activate it.

How do I maintain the starter long-term?

Once active, feed it 1:1:1 (starter : flour : water) every 12–24 hours at room temperature, or about once a week if you keep it in the fridge. Cared for this way, a starter can live for years.

Is sourdough gluten-free?

No. Sourdough made with wheat flour contains gluten and is not safe for people with celiac disease. If you have a gluten sensitivity, check with your doctor before trying it.
