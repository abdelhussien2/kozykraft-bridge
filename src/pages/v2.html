Why we tried it.

We kept hearing the same question: does a dried starter actually come back to life, or do you end up with a jar of paste? So we bought one and kept a log. One packet, one jar, a rubber band to mark the level, and a spot on the counter.

Day 1: Wake it up.

Emptied the whole packet into the jar, stirred in 1.5 tablespoons of filtered water, and left it for an hour. Then 1 tablespoon of flour, stirred to a thick paste, lid resting loosely on top. Marked the level with the rubber band.

PHOTO PLACEHOLDER · DAY 2Nothing yet · /images/diary/day-2.jpg

Day 2: Nothing yet. That's normal.

Fed it 2 tablespoons of flour and 1.5 tablespoons of water. No bubbles, no rise. The pouch says this is expected, so we kept going. This is the day most people quit.

PHOTO PLACEHOLDER · DAY 3First bubbles · /images/diary/day-3.jpg

Day 3: First bubbles.

Same feed. Small bubbles on the surface and a faint sour smell. It's alive.

PHOTO PLACEHOLDER · DAY 4The big feed, bigger jar · /images/diary/day-4.jpg

Day 4: The big feed, and a bigger jar.

A third of a cup of flour and a quarter cup of water. It needed a bigger jar after this one.

PHOTO PLACEHOLDER · DAY 5Doubled past the rubber band · /images/diary/day-5.jpg

Day 5: It doubled.

Fed it in the morning; by afternoon it was well past the rubber band, domed, and full of bubbles. That's the ready signal.

PHOTO PLACEHOLDER · DAY 6Photo of the crumb · /images/diary/day-6.jpg

Day 6: First loaf.

100 grams of starter into a basic Dutch oven loaf.

PHOTO PLACEHOLDER · DAY 7Verdict · /images/diary/day-7.jpg

Day 7: Verdict.

It works, and the part that matters is day 2. If you know a quiet day is normal, you don't throw it out. If you don't, you do. Everything you need to know is printed on the pouch, and there's a day-7 guarantee if yours doesn't bubble.
